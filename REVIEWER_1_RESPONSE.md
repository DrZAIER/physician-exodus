## 3. Comments on Author's Rebuttal to Reviewer 2 (Policy & Implementation Perspective)

Reviewer 2 provided a rigorous micro-econometric review from a purist decision-analytic perspective. As a health policy and systems specialist, I evaluate how the author's response to Reviewer 2 affects the paper's real-world relevance:

1. **Reframing Pseudo-RUM to a Macro-Policy Elasticity Framework (Critique 3.1)**:
   The authors made the correct scientific choice to stop describing their policy response functions as an individual utility-maximizing Discrete Choice Model. By designating it as a **Calibrated Macro-Policy Proportional Hazard / Elasticity Multiplier Framework**, they preserve all the valuable simulation mechanics while establishing total econometric humility. This protects the paper against academic purists without losing an ounce of policy relevance.
2. **Harmonizing the SCEN_5 Emigration Leakage Paradox (Critique 3.2)**:
   Reviewer 2’s discovery that SCEN_5 results in a median of $-904$ emigrants averted in the PSA was a critical catch. The authors' response—fully acknowledging this number and using it to demonstrate that **training expansion inherently leaks human capital abroad unless paired with 100% bonded retention**—is a masterclass in scientific integrity. It strengthens the paper's central message: supply expansion alone is not a substitute for health system retention.
3. **Health Economic Ratios & Ethical Dominance (Critique 3.3 & 5.3)**:
   Reviewer 2 criticized ICERs of 509k–3.3M TND as detached from GDP/capita. The authors correctly clarified the fundamental distinction between **cost per DALY** (single patient outcome) and **cost per physician-year** (annual institutional capacity delivering thousands of patient interventions). By elevating Budget Impact Analysis (BIA) and Cost-Consequence Analysis (CCA) in natural units to the primary presentation and formally excluding Scenario 4 from the frontier via **Ethical Dominance**, the authors satisfy both health economists and government budget directors.
4. **Calibration Expansion ($df = 5$) and Holdout Validation (Critique 3.4)**:
   Expanding the calibration baseline to 2015–2021 ($N = 7$) successfully provides $df = 5$ for estimating the 2 global scalar parameters, resolving the $df = 0$ critique. Their defense of the 27.91% holdout flow MAPE as reflecting an unprecedented post-crisis inflection in an LMIC setting is completely reasonable, especially given their tight 6.88% holdout stock MAPE and strong correlation with French ($r = 0.871$) and German ($r = 0.933$) registers.
5. **Continuous-Time Markov vs. Tunnel States (Critique 4.1)**:
   The authors rightly defended their continuous-time Markov aggregation. In an LMIC setting like Tunisia where longitudinal age-by-residency micro-panels do not exist, attempting to force 180+ tunnel states would have created severe matrix sparsity and pseudo-precision. The sensitivity bounds provided in `ASSUMPTIONS.md` are more than sufficient.
