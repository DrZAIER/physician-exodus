Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"Retention, return or training? A stock-and-flow simulation of Tunisia's physician workforce under incomplete migration data"**, for consideration as a Research article in *Human Resources for Health*.

**Why this manuscript fits the journal.** Tunisia is a high-emigration, middle-income country whose physician workforce debate is conducted with incompatible numbers: registered physicians (27 935 in 2022), practising physicians (15 315 in 2024) and reported annual departures (187 removal requests in the first half of 2023 versus 1 300–1 450 press-reported departures) are routinely conflated. The manuscript addresses a core HRH theme — health workforce mobility and migration — and does so in a way that is directly relevant to other middle-income countries with incomplete migration data.

**What we did.** We developed a transparent, reproducible, annual cohort-component model of physicians in active practice in Tunisia, calibrated exactly to the 2024 national practising stock, with 2017–2024 used as a retrospective comparison. The model represents entry after postgraduate training, emigration before and after entry, return migration from a return-eligible diaspora pool with attrition, retirement and mortality. We compared business as usual with five policy scenarios: remuneration and working conditions, career development, return migration, expanded training, and a combined retention and return strategy. We used 2 000 Monte Carlo draws with exact calibration, true partial rank correlations, alternative effect sizes, separate structural trends on the pre- and post-entry emigration margins, a break-even analysis and illustrative costs.

**What we found.** The observed stock path is consistent with 582 to 1 497 annual emigrants; a destination-stock check narrows this to 582–1 405. The emigration level is therefore not identifiable from aggregate data. The baseline is a plateau, not a collapse: 16 019 physicians in 2040 (1.26 per 1 000), close to the 2024 level. Within 15 years, a combined retention and return strategy adds 5 106 physicians by 2040; remuneration adds 3 485, career development 1 741, return migration 1 301 and a 30% training expansion 1 112. A 9.6% reduction in emigration matches the 30% training expansion, whereas training would need to rise 94% to match a 30% retention effect. The combined strategy remains above business as usual if emigration rises, but the absolute level depends on whether the trend applies to post-entry emigration only or to both margins.

**Why it matters.** The paper does not claim to predict the future or to estimate causal policy effects. Its contribution is to make the trade-offs explicit, to show which assumptions drive the conclusions, and to identify the data that would reduce uncertainty. It also offers a transferable methodological approach for countries where migration flows are poorly observed.
